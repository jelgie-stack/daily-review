# Unit drop: one or a few units moved against their own baseline

The brief already compared each unit to itself, so "it is a small store" is not an explanation.

## Checks, in order

1. **Did it report at all?** A unit with partial-day data looks like a 40% drop. Check the time
   of the last order yesterday vs a normal day. If the last order was at 2pm, the feed stopped.
2. **Hours and closures.** Closed early, opened late, reduced hours, a power cut, a health
   inspection. Ask the unit before you analyze anything.
3. **Local causes.** Weather at that location, road closures, a nearby event that ended, a
   competitor opening. One unit down and its neighbours fine usually means something physical.
4. **Pattern across days.** The "yesterday too?" column is the tell. One day: watch. Two days:
   call the operator. A week: it is the new baseline and the question is why.
5. **New units.** A unit less than ~12 weeks old has a baseline that is still climbing, so a
   "drop" may just be a flat week. Treat new-unit moves as information, not alarms, and keep
   them out of the core numbers if they dominate.

## Deciding

Only the persistent ones get assigned. For one-off drops, add a line to the brief and move on;
the review tomorrow will say whether it held.
